Two CTAs, one primary
"Try it first" is the secondary button but it's what most new users will tap — no commitment, no friction. SIWA is there for users already convinced. Both paths converge at the same recipe cards; the only difference is whether their votes are saved.
Tutorial timing
The 2.5s auto-demo runs immediately with no skip button. After the demo resolves, real cards begin. The tooltip disappears after the first real swipe. No explanation screens. The mechanic is self-evident from watching it.
Partner hint — Option A
Starting from card 2, every card shows a dimmed "?" avatar pill in the top-right corner: "Invite to vote." Persistent but quiet. Plants the idea without interrupting flow. The real persuasion comes on card 3.

Ghost vote — animation spec
The ghost card sits underneath the top card at rest. After the 3rd real vote commits, a 600ms sequence plays: ghost card slides out from behind, auto-drags right (approve direction), green tint fades in, "✓ Approved" badge appears, card flies off. The callout slides up 200ms after the card exits. No user interaction required.
The lock is a CTA, not a wall
The menu preview is fully visible — they can see exactly what they built. The lock only blocks saving and finalizing. This is intentional: showing the completed state creates desire. Locking an empty screen creates frustration.
Sunk cost framing
The save screen lists their actual voted recipes. "Don't lose your picks" speaks to loss aversion. Their work is shown back to them as something worth preserving — this is the most effective moment to ask for an account.

Annual pre-selected
The annual plan is selected by default. The savings badge ("Save 33%") makes switching to monthly feel like a downgrade. Monthly is there for price-sensitive users — it anchors the annual as the obvious choice.
Land mid-action, not on empty
Home shows their actual menu already in progress. They didn't arrive at a blank state — they arrived at work they recognize. The "3 recipes approved" hero card references the voting session they just finished. Continuity = confidence the app works.
Persistent invite nudge
The ghost vote planted the idea; now the home screen keeps it warm. The nudge is dismissible and lives in the home feed — not a modal. It stays until they either invite someone or explicitly dismiss it. Should not re-appear after dismissal.
Recipe selection for onboarding
The cards shown during onboarding must be your best-performing public recipes — highest approval rate in the database, filtered for visual appeal (good food photography). Showing a mediocre recipe during a user's first 60 seconds is conversion suicide. Consider a curated "onboarding pool" of 12–15 top-rated recipes that never changes without deliberate review.
Ghost partner name
The ghost avatar shows "C" for Claire in these mockups, but in production the name should be generic unless the user arrived via an invite link (in which case, use the inviter's actual name). Default: use a common first initial — "A", "C", "J" — that feels plausible without being unsettling. Do not use the user's own initial.
Guest session persistence
Votes cast before sign-in must survive the auth flow. Store them locally in SharedPreferences keyed by a device-scoped guest ID. On sign-in, migrate guest votes to the new user's account server-side. The user should never notice their votes were temporarily local — the menu preview they saw before signup must match exactly after.
Skip-paywall path
"Skip for now" on the paywall drops them to the home screen with a 7-day trial already started. The skip does not end the trial — it just defers the confirmation screen. This prevents users from bouncing off the paywall entirely. The trial is automatic on account creation; the paywall screen is just the reveal moment.
